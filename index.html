What a five-page small-business WordPress site should include before it launches

A first website for a service business does not need a large blog. It needs a small set of pages a customer can use, and a public URL that behaves the way you think it does. This is the pre-launch list I use for a simple five-page WordPress site.

The five pages

Most service businesses can start with these:

  1. A homepage that names the business, the service, and the area, and that points to a clear next step.
  2. One service page, or a short set of service pages, each focused on work the business actually sells.
  3. An about page that says who does the work and where they are based, without filler.
  4. A contact or quote page with a working form, the phone number, and the email address.
  5. One useful article only if there is a real customer question to answer — for example, how long a job takes, or what to prepare before a visit. If there is no such question yet, skip the article and launch with four pages rather than publishing a thin post.

A single "Services" dump for every offer is usually weaker than one clear page per service you are ready to take on.

What each page has to do

The homepage and the service page should say what you do in the first screen, not after a long story. The contact path should be obvious: a button, a form, and the phone and email you actually answer. For a Fort Lauderdale service-area business, name the city you cover, and keep a street address off the public pages if you do not serve walk-in customers there.

Technical checks that belong on the public URL

Before you call the site launched, open the public URLs in a signed-out browser and with a header check. The homepage, the service page, and the contact page should return HTTP 200 at the canonical host. They should not send noindex in a meta tag or in an X-Robots-Tag header. The contact form should submit. The XML sitemap should list the live pages, and those URLs should not 404 or redirect to an unexpected place.

These checks are unglamorous, and they are where a month of build work can be wasted if a plugin or a cache starts telling search engines to stay away.

What this is not

This list is not a ranking promise, and it is not a quote for a build. It is the baseline that should be true before a five-page site is treated as live. A later redesign or a larger content plan can wait until this foundation is in place.
